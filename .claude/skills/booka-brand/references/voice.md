# Voice and approved claims

## The voice

Concrete, declarative, unhedged. The reader is a buyer who has been pitched
before and can smell padding.

**Name the cost before naming the fix.** The opening of the landing page
spends its first paragraph entirely on what the problem costs, and only then
introduces the product. That order is deliberate — a solution stated before
the reader feels the problem reads as a brochure.

**Short sentences carry the claims.** Long ones can carry context. When a
sentence is doing the arguing, cut it back until nothing is left but the
argument: "We never appear." "Month three is faster than month one." "We know,
because we did it."

**One emphasis per paragraph.** Bold the sentence carrying the claim, never a
scattering of keywords. Everything bold is the same as nothing bold. Emphasis
is weight *and* ink colour — see the type rules.

**Specific beats clever.** "Twenty-four months to place one professional"
beats "glacial timelines". A number a reader can check is worth more than an
adjective they can dismiss.

Avoid: exclamation marks, "revolutionary", "seamless", "cutting-edge",
"game-changing", "unlock", "leverage" as a verb, and any sentence that would
survive being pasted onto a competitor's site unchanged.

## British or American spelling

The landing page uses British forms in body copy ("optimising", "centre",
"licence" as a noun). Keep whichever a document starts with, consistently.

## Approved claims — use verbatim

These are measured. Rounding one up to something punchier is how a deck ends
up making a claim the company can't defend in a sales call.

| Claim | Exact wording | Context that must travel with it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Acceptance | **94%** first-submission acceptance rate | Industry standard for manually-prepared files is closer to 60%. Every rejection costs a resubmission cycle: 6 to 12 weeks. |
| Time | **85%** reduction in processing time | From 24 months to under 4. Measured across 2,500+ completed files — measured, not modelled. |
| Coverage | **11 regulators. 10 countries. 1 integration.** | SCFHS, DHA, DOH, QCHP, MOH Oman, GMC, NMC, DREETS, Spain's Ministry of Universities, and more. |
| Network | **100,000+** verified professionals | Already in the network. You're plugging into a pipeline that already exists, not starting one. |
| Origin markets | **15+** source countries processed | Latin America, South Asia, North Africa. The engine doesn't care where they trained. |
| Build cost | Three years and a seven-figure investment to replicate internally | Weeks to license. |
| Idle cost | Nine thousand dollars a day | Burned on every unfilled seat. |

Where a number is an estimate rather than a measurement, say so in the same
breath. "Measured across 2,500+ completed files, not modelled" does more for
credibility than the number alone.

## The positioning, in one paragraph

Booka is white-label credentialing and licensing infrastructure. Clients
deploy the engine inside their own organisation under their own brand; their
candidates and their clients never see Booka. Underneath, AI agents build a
knowledge base specific to that client — their processes, criteria, target
markets and regulators — living inside their workflows rather than in a
separate tool, and improving with every file processed. Origin market stops
mattering: credentials are validated against the destination regulator
regardless of where the professional trained.

## The four things it changes for a buyer

Use these as the spine of any deck. They're the argument, in order:

1. **Cost per hire drops.** The vendor stack is retired — agencies, legal
   hours, translation, coordination overhead. One system absorbs the chain.
2. **Operational load disappears.** The team stops managing documents and
   starts managing outcomes. Nothing gets filed until it's clean.
3. **Time-to-productivity collapses.** Twenty-four months becomes weeks. Every
   month pulled forward is a month of revenue that wasn't being captured.
4. **A new revenue line.** Not only a cost centre being optimised — a
   capability that can be sold, on the client's own terms, at their margin.

Close on the build-or-license choice: building it internally takes three
years, a regulatory legal team, direct relationships with eleven authorities,
and a document corpus large enough to train on. Or go live in weeks.

The closing line of the landing page is: **You sell. We execute. Nobody sees
us.**

## Trust and security

Every verified credential is hashed and written to an immutable ledger, so a
regulator, client or auditor can prove a file hasn't changed since the day it
cleared. Documents are encrypted in transit and at rest, isolated per
organisation, never pooled across clients. Access is governed per role with
approval flows and locks.

## Naming

- **Booka**, not BOOKA or booka.
- The primary call to action is **Booka Demo**.
- Regulators by their acronym on first use with the full name if the audience
  is unlikely to know it — SCFHS, DHA, GMC, NMC, DREETS, QCHP.
- **Epic is not a regulator.** It's an electronic health record vendor. It has
  been mistakenly grouped with regulator logos before; if it belongs in a
  deck, label it as an integration, not an authority.
